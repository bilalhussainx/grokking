# KairosLearn Research Corpus — Part 1: Competitors and What Students Say (2026–27 cycle)

No competitor verified here offers a fully agentic, student-side counselor with memory across sessions that also covers US, UK and Canadian applications and transfer students end to end. The AI tools are mostly essay reviewers or chat-plus-chancing funnels that answer when asked. The human firms put strong accountability behind five- and six-figure fees that most families cannot pay. KairosLearn's clearest opening is a free or low-cost agent that reaches out to students first and plans across systems.

## TL;DR
- **AI tools cluster at two poles, and neither is agentic.** CollegeVine Sage, Kollegio and KapAdvisor are free chat tools; Kolly charges $29/month or $149 per 6 months. None of them documents taking actions for the student, such as submitting, scheduling or editing inside the application. The one clearly agentic, student-side integration found is College Possible's Coach Possible inside Common App (announced June 16, 2026), and only students in College Possible's advising program can use it.
- **Human firms earn strong reviews but are priced out of reach and opaque.** Crimson rates 4.8/5 on Trustpilot (427 reviews). InGenius Prep rates 4.9 (248) and Command Education 4.1 (7). None publishes package prices; third-party figures put Crimson at $25,000 and up, and Command founder Christopher Rim cites $85,000 per year for its Premier Roadmap program (The Telegraph reported $120,000 a year in 2025). The negative reviews repeat three complaints: rigid refund terms, turnover or unresponsive senior advisors, and generic advice at a premium price.
- **The white space is proactive, cross-system and equity-first.** The sourced products are built around US selective admissions: T20 essay calibration, SAT/ACT, US chancing and US college networks. Almost none document UK (UCAS), Ontario (OUAC) or transfer workflows. None, apart from coach-mediated Coach Possible, is designed to reach a shy student who never asks for help.

### New entrants 2025–26 (AI agents, essay tools, application assistants)
- **Kolly 2.0 (launched May 9, 2026).** Kolly calls it a rebuild "around our own model, trained on hundreds of thousands of essays from students admitted to top schools," with a product "that reads the whole application the way an admissions officer does."\[8\]
- **Coach Possible in Common App (announced June 16, 2026).** College Possible, which serves "more than 25,000 students annually" and focuses on Pell-eligible students, integrated its AI advising tool into Common App. It gives 24/7 support, and coaches can "view student activity and identify where additional support may be needed." CEO Siva Kumari: "Information is not the problem these days—it's the human connection and the motivation to do the right things at the right time."\[22\]
- **Common App + EdVisorly.** This is a pilot of AI transcript processing in Texas aimed at low- and middle-income students.\[39\] It is institution-side, not a student agent.
- **Jenova "College Admissions Consultant."** It markets "persistent memory" and "real-time research capabilities."\[40\] Its claims appear only on Jenova's own site; independent verification is UNVERIFIED.
- **IvyClaw.** It runs as a local "skill" on the open-source OpenClaw agent framework and claims "over 320,000 real offer records spanning 2021 to 2026."\[41\] The source is a paid press release in National Law Review's press-release section; all performance claims are UNVERIFIED.
- **Trajecta.** A staged AI "Committee Review" of the whole application, priced at $24.99/month or $249.99/year, with a free tier. The figures come from Trajecta's own comparison page.\[42\]
- **Orbit (Solvi), Counselly, Instudi, Apply Genius.** These appear only in competitor-authored roundups: Orbit's own blog and Jenova's blog.\[1\]\[11\]\[43\] Their pricing and features are UNVERIFIED pending their official pages.
- **Blackbaud Admissions Agent (previewed July 16, 2026).** It is institution-side, for independent K–12 school admissions, and "works semi-autonomously or fully autonomously."\[44\] It does not compete for college applicants, but it shows where agentic admissions tooling is heading.
- **CollegeVine AI Recruiter.** This is institution-side "agentic AI" for colleges. CollegeVine's July 24, 2024 Business Wire release reported its first 50 partners, with CEO Zack Perkins saying "Fifty partners in two months is an amazing milestone"; an October 30, 2024 release put it at "over 95 partner institutions." Students on CollegeVine's forum are skeptical of it (see complaints below).\[45\]

## Details

### Why these two independent consultants
The brief called for two well-reviewed independent consultants with a clearly documented review base. **Signature College Counseling** (Elizabeth Levine, NY) was selected because it is an IECA-affiliated independent practice that publicly claims "220+ 5 Star Google reviews" and publishes named testimonials.\[38\]\[46\] Caveats: its team includes "2 College Counselors, 6 Essay Coaches" and more, so it is small but not solo;\[38\] the Google count is self-reported, and IECA membership was not checked in IECA's directory. A second qualifying consultant could not be verified in this pass, so it is marked `UNVERIFIED`. International College Counselors was examined but rejected: it is a multi-counselor firm with only 10 Trustpilot reviews (4.4/5), and Trustpilot flags "No recent history of asking for reviews."\[47\] The IECA and HECA member directories, cross-checked against Google Business profiles, would settle the second pick.

### Review-base reliability notes
- CollegeVine's Trustpilot profile is small and stale: 64 reviews, only 1 in the last 12 months, and the company "Hasn't replied to negative reviews".\[3\] Most of it concerns the older paid mentoring and SAT products, not Sage.
- Crimson "invites their customers to review," which tends to raise scores.\[27\] Command Education's 7 reviews are too few to generalize from. InGenius Prep "hasn't invited customers recently."\[29\]
- Kollegio, Kolly, KapAdvisor, Scoir and PowerBuddy have no independent student reviews in this pass. The quotes used for them are company-curated and labeled as such. App Store and Google Play review text for Scoir, Naviance and the Crimson App would settle this. A third-party site (Marlvel) reports the Crimson app at 4.2/5 from 2.6K reviews;\[48\] that is UNVERIFIED until checked against Google Play and the App Store.

### Pricing conflicts flagged
- **Kolly.** The pricing page fetched on September 27, 2026 lists $29/month and "$149 / 6 months… Cancel anytime before it renews."\[7\] An earlier indexed version of the same page described the Season Pass as "a one-time charge with no auto-renewal."\[7\] Trajecta's comparison page cites "$39 a month."\[42\] Use the live pricing page as authoritative; the terms appear to have changed during 2026.
- **Crimson.** Third-party estimates range from "$15,000 to over $100,000" (Orbit) to "upwards of USD $25,000, with some families paying close to USD $200,000" (Wikipedia).\[23\]\[49\] There is no official price list. PrepScholar reports Crimson essay review "from $70 for a single essay to $1,500 for a 20 essay bundle";\[50\] that figure is UNVERIFIED against Crimson's own shop.

## Recommendations

White-space opportunities for an agentic AI counselor serving US, UK and Canada applicants, drawn only from the evidence above:

1. **Be proactive, not reactive: the shy-student gap.** Every consumer AI tool here waits for the student to ask. The only sourced product that surfaces inactive students, Coach Possible, needs a human coach and is limited to College Possible's program. Kumari's diagnosis applies to KairosLearn directly: "Information is not the problem… it's… the motivation to do the right things at the right time."\[22\] KairosLearn should nudge students first, detect stalls, and tell students what matters "right now."
2. **Make deadlines and facts verifiable, then act on them.** CollegeVine's documented wrong Dartmouth ED II deadline shows the trust cost of unverified data.\[70\] An agent that live-checks official admissions pages and cites them, then schedules reminders and calendar holds, answers the most concrete AI complaint found.
3. **Cover UK, Canada and transfer, which are almost entirely missing.** Kolly calibrates on US T20 essays, Naviance draws on US federal data, and KapAdvisor is built around SAT/ACT. Among the sourced options, only Crimson (human, five figures) covers the UK and Canada.\[71\] No sourced tool documents UCAS, OUAC or transfer workflows. That combination is uncontested.
4. **Reproduce the human-firm benefits reviewers praise, without the complaints.** Praise centers on accountability and continuity: "managed the application process… so we didn't have to nudge him," regular check-ins, and emotional support. Complaints center on turnover ("emails kept bouncing back"), slow replies ("within 72 hours"),\[28\]\[29\] generic advice and refund traps. Persistent memory, instant replies and no lock-in contracts address each one.
5. **Be equity-first and non-extractive.** CollegeVine's free tier is paid for by college recruiting,\[43\] and students call the outreach spam ("AI spambots ruin everything"). Kolly and KapAdvisor put depth behind paywalls. A counselor funded without selling student attention, that states which aid and cost questions it answers, has room to win first-generation and low-income trust.
6. **Stay inside integrity lines.** Common App's Fraud Policy, as quoted on Brown University's Integrity in the Application Process page, counts as fraud "intentionally misrepresenting as one's own original work... the substantive content or output of an artificial intelligence platform, technology, or algorithm." KapAdvisor and Kolly both advertise "guide, not replace."\[13\] KairosLearn's document-editing actions should be suggestion-only, with the student confirming each change.

## Caveats
- Several roundup sources, including Orbit/findmyorbit.com, Jenova, Trajecta and bestcollegeadmissionconsultants.com, are published by competitors or affiliate-style review sites. They were used only for leads and third-party price estimates, which are flagged as such.
- Trustpilot counts, page positions and TrustScores change daily. Crimson's count appeared as anywhere from 425 to 430 across pages in the same session.\[26\]\[27\] Recheck before any public use.
- Two Crimson complaint quotes and one InGenius complaint quote come from Trustpilot search-result text for the stated page URLs, not from fetched pages; review pagination may have moved.
- The access date for every item is September 27, 2026, per the task brief.
